# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Three people use Chorus during one live event, each on a different screen:

- **The host** (an event agency or organizer, Sense Conseil first): prepares interactions on a laptop before the event and runs them live, often under time pressure, from the dashboard or the projector.
- **The room** (participants at congresses, conferences and trainings, e.g. Djazair Dental Congress, a few hundred people): joins in seconds with a code or QR on their own phone, often on venue Wi-Fi, and answers polls, quizzes and Q&A while listening to a speaker.
- **The big screen** (projected for everyone): shows the join code, the lobby, live results and the quiz, and must read from the back of a hall.

Users read French, English and Arabic (right-to-left).

## Product Purpose

Chorus turns a talk into a two-way exchange: live polls, word clouds, ratings, rankings, open text, upvoted Q&A, surveys and Kahoot-style quizzes, with a lobby before each interaction and a shareable report afterwards. Success is a room where most people take part without help, a host who never waits on the tool, and a report a sponsor wants to read.

## Brand Personality

Professional, calm, trustworthy. The audience is often medical and corporate; the tool should feel serious and reliable, never noisy. Energy comes from the room's participation (results filling, names arriving), not from decoration.

## Anti-references

- Not a copy of Slido or Kahoot: Chorus has its own brand and never reuses their assets.

## Design Principles

1. **The room before the interface.** Every screen is read in a live setting: large, legible, unambiguous, usable in seconds.
2. **Never make anyone wait on the tool.** Taps answer instantly; loading never blanks the screen.
3. **One action per moment.** Each screen has an obvious next step (join, answer, start, reveal).
4. **Calm confidence.** Restraint in color and motion; motion only where it explains a change.
5. **Equal in three languages.** French, English and Arabic (RTL) are first-class, not translations bolted on.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Working assumptions (not yet confirmed): aim for WCAG 2.2 AA; projector-legible text and contrast; full right-to-left support for Arabic; respect reduced-motion preferences; touch targets sized for phones used one-handed.
